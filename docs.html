Overview

Fresh Snacks is a Firebase-backed snack tab app: customers log purchases and clear their balance, admins manage the catalog, payments, and promotions. Firebase Auth handles identity (anonymous guests for customers, Google/Microsoft/email for admins); all reads/writes go through a Cloud Functions Express API (functions/src/routes/) rather than the client talking to Firestore directly - the client-side js/firebase-store.js / js/firebase-admin.js are thin fetch() wrappers around it.

This page documents how things work, not just what's in the UI - the parts an admin can't see just by clicking around (rules, thresholds, edge cases). It's meant to grow - ask for more sections any time a mechanism isn't covered yet.

Early-payment cashback

functions/src/lib/cashback.js - a customer who clears their entire balance to $0 earns a percentage of what just cleared, credited back as a new payment they can spend on future snacks.

The per-margin tier rule

Every still-owed purchase is judged on its own createdDate, not the balance as a whole - a fresh charge added on top of an old, already-expired one still earns its own rate:

Days since that charge was addedRate
0 (same calendar day)10%
1 (next day)5%
2+ days0% - permanently missed

Gates that must all pass

  • Whole-balance clearance - the action must bring every still-owed charge to $0, not just the one it directly touches.
  • $300 floor (MIN_BALANCE_FOR_CASHBACK_JMD) - a trivial balance never triggers a payout.
  • Launch date (LAUNCH_DATE = "2026-07-29") - a balance that opened before this date is never eligible, no matter the math.
  • Daily bonus window - 7am-3pm business-local (UTC-5). The settlement itself (the moment the balance actually hits $0) must land inside this window, or the reward is $0 even on an otherwise same-day balance. This mirrors index.html's own "draining bar" countdown, which had been purely cosmetic before 2026-08-19 - see the code comment at the top of cashback.js for the history.
  • No pre-existing credit involved - see below.

The credit ledger (no discount on a discount)

A customer's unspent cashback reward is tracked explicitly, not just inferred from totals:

  • Every payout is a payments doc with source: "cashback" - an earn event.
  • Every time a later settlement draws on that unspent reward to help clear a balance, a creditConsumptions/{id} doc is written - a consume event.
  • creditBalance(payments, consumptions) = sum(earned) - sum(consumed). This is what's shown as creditBalance in /admin/snapshot's per-customer accounting row and in the customer's own /store/data response.
All-or-nothing, not prorated. If a settlement round draws on any existing credit at all, that round earns zero new bonus - even if most of the money was fresh. This replaced an earlier prorated version after a real incident (a customer's second payment round re-earned cashback on money that was actually her own already-earned credit, not new money).

Key functions: marginCashback() (the gross per-margin tier math, no credit awareness), evaluateCashback() (the real payout decision - transactions/payments/consumptions before the action, plus which ids just settled), projectedCashback() (the customer-facing "if you paid right now" card - deliberately NOT gated on the daily window, since index.html runs its own live countdown for that).

Settlement & payment allocation

functions/src/lib/shared.js's paymentAllocationPlan(transactions, paidTotal) is the oldest-first settlement engine every payment path shares. Eligible statuses (SETTLEMENT_ELIGIBLE): PENDING_USER_CONFIRMATION, CONFIRMED_UNPAID, PAYMENT_PENDING_ADMIN_CONFIRMATION - sorted oldest createdDate first, each fully settled in turn until the money runs out. Whatever's left over becomes the customer's spendable credit.

functions/src/lib/settlement.js's allocateApprovedTransactions(userId, actorUid, freshPaymentIds) runs that plan for real: finalizes every settled id (via the correct workflow action - CONFIRM_PAYMENT if it was already a reported payment claim, MARK_AS_PAID otherwise), then checks evaluateCashback against the pre-sweep snapshot.

Called from five places: the two admin payment routes (POST /admin/payments/permanent, PayPal capture), the reconcile sweep (POST /admin/payments/reconcile), and two auto-settle triggers in store.js (right after a customer adds a purchase, and right after a payment claim is marked paid) - a customer who already has enough credit on file never has to separately wait for "mark as paid."

freshPaymentIds exists so a payment written immediately before calling this (the two routes above) doesn't get double-counted as "pre-existing credit" against its own settlement round - only those two call sites ever pass it.

Balance & accounting

accounting(users, devices, transactions, payments, adjustments) in shared.js rolls every customer into one row: balance = snackTotal + adjustmentTotal - paidTotal. ITEM_UNDER_REVIEW transactions are excluded from snackTotal (a live dispute isn't counted as owed until an admin resolves it) - every other non-final status counts, including one still awaiting the customer's own confirmation.

This is a flat sum, not a per-transaction ledger - it's why a stray old credit (e.g. unspent cashback) automatically nets against a brand-new unpaid balance the moment both exist, with no extra step needed to "apply" it.

PayPal tab clearing

functions/src/routes/paypalTab.js - the real, authenticated "clear my tab with PayPal" flow (separate from paypal.js, which only backs the standalone unauthenticated demo page). The amount charged is always the caller's own real balance computed server-side, never a number the client sends.

paypalOrders/{orderId} anchors the whole flow so a payment can never settle twice: status moves created → capturing → captured → settled, and every step is safe to retry from wherever it left off - a repeated capture call just returns the already-settled result instead of charging again.

RouteWhat it does
GET /paypal/tab/quoteReal balance + today's exchange rate + the 5% processing fee breakdown.
POST /paypal/tab/create-orderCreates the PayPal order, writes paypalOrders at status: "created".
POST /paypal/tab/capture-orderCaptures the charge, writes the payments doc (JMD, source: "paypal"), calls allocateApprovedTransactions, marks the order settled.

Transaction workflow

functions/src/lib/transactionStatus.js is the single authority on what status follows what - every route calls assertTransition() instead of hand-rolling status checks.

StatusMeaning
PENDING_USER_CONFIRMATIONAdmin-added, waiting on the customer to confirm it's theirs.
ITEM_UNDER_REVIEWLive dispute - excluded from balance until an admin resolves it.
CONFIRMED_UNPAIDOwed, confirmed, not yet paid.
PAYMENT_PENDING_ADMIN_CONFIRMATIONCustomer reported paying; needs an admin to confirm.
PAYMENT_UNDER_REVIEWAn admin put a reported payment claim under active scrutiny.
PAID_FINALIZEDTerminal. No transition out, for any role or action - audit-trail integrity by design.
CANCELLEDTerminal. Also sets the legacy status: "void" field.
There is no "un-finalize" action. Correcting a wrongly-paid transaction means voiding the payment(s) involved (POST /admin/payments/:id/void) and, if the underlying purchase itself needs to be reopened, deleting and re-adding the transaction record - not trying to force it backward through the state machine.

availableActions(workflowStatus, role) is what every "what buttons show here" UI (transactions.html's row actions, the notification panel's primary action) is supposed to read from, rather than assuming a fixed action per status.

Promotional QR tracking

functions/src/lib/promoScans.js + sitemap.html's "Promotions" section. A promo QR lands on an existing page (currently always index.html) with two query params, no dedicated landing page:

index.html?promo=hydrate&qr=D8SFN8
  • promo - the campaign code (e.g. hydrate). Shared across every physical placement of that campaign.
  • qr - a short unique id minted per generated QR code (POST /admin/promo-links), so different printed placements of the same campaign stay distinguishable in the data even though they land on the same page.

Two Firestore collections: promoLinks/{qrId} (one doc per generated/printed code - promoCode, label, url) and promoScans/{scanId} (one doc per landing that arrived carrying a code). GET /admin/promo-links joins them to show a live scan count per code on the Sitemap page.

On the client: index.html reads ?promo/?qr once at boot, strips them from the URL immediately via history.replaceState (so a reload never double-counts), and passes them through FS.loadData(promo, qr) to GET /store/data, which fires the scan record best-effort. A promo landing also auto-opens the name-capture modal once per visit (closing it doesn't reopen it) - the same modal a followed device-link invite auto-opens, just gated on a different condition.

Scaffolding for a future in-app scanner: recordPromoScan() takes a scannedVia field, always "url" today (a browser just following a link). A future scanner built into the app (an already-signed-in customer scanning a code from inside Fresh Snacks itself, not landing fresh) is expected to call the same function with scannedVia: "in-app" and the real, already-known uid doing the scanning - no schema change needed when that ships, just a second call site.

Device linking & tab codes

Every share code lives in codes/{CODE} with a type: "view" (a read-mostly capability code, e.g. a customer sharing their tab) or "link" (a device-linking invite). A customer's own claim on a code lives in claims/{uid}.

RouteWhat it does
POST /store/claim/resolveResolve a view code - lets this browser see (not necessarily edit) another tab.
POST /store/link/inviteSelf-service: whoever already has full access to a tab can fetch/create its link invite code.
POST /store/link/acceptPermanently link this device (linkedUids) - capped at 3 devices per tab, checked and updated in one Firestore transaction so a failed switch can't strand a device on neither tab.
POST /store/link/sessionA 4th+ device falls back to a temporary session claim (accessMode: "session") instead of a permanent slot, once the 3-device cap is hit.
POST /store/link/unlinkRemoves this device from linkedUids. The claim doc itself is kept (as de-link history), not deleted.

functions/src/lib/authz.js re-implements every access check firestore.rules used to enforce (the Admin SDK bypasses rules entirely, so this is the real gate now). canAccessTab(uid, ownerId) is the one function almost everything else calls: true for the owner, an admin, a view-code claim, a linked device, or an active invite session - checked in that order.

Admin roles & permissions

functions/src/lib/permissions.js. Every sensitive action is gated by one of ten PERMISSION keys, checked against the calling admin's own admins/{uid}.permissions map.

Grandfathering: an admin doc with no permissions map at all gets full access - only admins created through the admin-management screen get an explicit map, and only a key explicitly set to false in it is ever denied. A permission added in a later release doesn't retroactively lock out anyone.
PresetCan't do
adminEverything, including managing other admins.
accountingDelete a transaction; manage other admins. Everything else, including cancel/void.
cashierAnything that edits, disputes, cancels, or deletes. Can mark paid and confirm payment only.

Presets are just starting points for the toggle table in admin-users.html - the individual toggles on an admin's own doc are what's actually enforced.

Admin notifications

js/admin-notifications.js - the bell on every admin page. Two kinds of item: actionable backlog (feedback, disputed items, payment claims - resolves itself once the underlying record changes, or dismissible without acting) and activity notices (a name added, a self-checkout - no natural "resolved" state, stays until dismissed). Both count toward the bell's badge number.

The action button on a transaction row is driven by that transaction's real, server-computed availableActions (same source of truth as transactions.html's own buttons), not a hardcoded guess per status - it can never offer an action the backend would actually reject.

Freshness, three layers, no webhooks or live listeners needed for this:

  • Opening the panel refetches before rendering.
  • A 409 TRANSACTION_STATE_CHANGED from a stale click (someone else already resolved it) silently refreshes and re-renders instead of showing a dead-end error.
  • A 60-second background poll while any admin page is open, plus an explicit refresh call from transactions.html right after its own actions succeed.
